Chapter 1

Why AI Agent Teams?

AI is transforming everyday life and work. But a single AI struggles to solve complex problems. What happens when several AI agents cooperate, each with its own role? This chapter explains why AI agent teams are needed and what a harness is.

What This Chapter Covers
  • Recent AI development trends and how AI is being used at work
  • The difference between a single AI and a multi-agent team
  • The concept and role of a harness
  • The expected benefits of using agent teams
  • The overall roadmap of this handbook

Rapid AI Advances

Explosive Growth Since 2023

When ChatGPT was released in late 2022, artificial intelligence moved from an experimental technology inside research labs to a practical tool in everyday life. In 2023, powerful AI models such as Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini, and Meta's Llama were released one after another. In 2024, Claude 3.5 and GPT-4o appeared, bringing dramatic improvements in reasoning and multilingual capability. From 2025 into 2026, AI coding tools such as Claude Code, Antigravity CLI, and Cursor spread widely, reaching not only developers but general users as well.

The heart of this change is that AI is evolving beyond a simple "conversational AI" into an agent that acts on its own. AI of the past stopped at answering questions; today it can create files, write code, collect information from the web, and carry out multi-step tasks by itself.

Areas of Work AI Is Already Transforming

AI is already being used actively in the following areas of work.

  • Document writing — Reports, proposals, email drafts, and blog posts can be drafted and revised quickly. It has become standard practice for AI to produce a draft that the user reviews and refines.
  • Coding and development — With tools like Claude Code and GitHub Copilot, coding beginners can build simple programs, while experienced developers automate repetitive tasks and sharply increase productivity.
  • Data analysis — AI can extract key insights from large volumes of data and visualize them as charts and graphs.
  • Presentation creation — Systems have appeared in which you enter a topic and the AI automatically handles slide composition, content writing, and even design.
  • Research and investigation — Using web search and document summarization, research on a given topic can be completed in minutes.

Why Beginners Should Learn AI

AI tools are no longer "something only technical experts use." Writers, planners, teachers, students, freelancers — regardless of your occupation, the ability to use AI is now an essential skill. Coordinating multiple AI agents in particular goes beyond simply "using AI"; it amounts to "operating AI like a team." That is a core competency that dramatically improves work efficiency.

Tip: You can use AI tools without knowing how to code. Tools like the Claude Desktop App respond to natural-language instructions (in Korean or English), so people with no programming experience can make full use of them.

Single AI vs Agent Teams

This is the first question you face when using AI. "Isn't a single AI enough?" To give you the answer up front: for simple tasks, one AI is enough. But for complex projects — for instance, automating the entire process from market research to a finished presentation — an agent team, in which several AIs divide the roles and cooperate, is far more effective.

Comparison Diagram

Limitations of a Single AI

A single AI handles every task with one model. It suits answering simple questions or writing short pieces, but complex projects expose the following limitations.

  • Limits of the context window — An AI can process only a limited amount of information at once. Juggling several long documents and large data sets at the same time can make it forget or mix up earlier information. This limit is called the context window.
  • Lack of deep specialization — It is hard for one AI to perform research, design, coding, and document review all at a high level. Dividing the expert roles allows deeper expertise in each field.
  • No parallel work — A single AI performs one task at a time; research must finish before design begins. In real work environments, several tasks usually proceed at the same time.
  • Difficult quality control — The structure in which an AI reviews its own output makes it easy to miss biases and errors. Having a separate "reviewer" role greatly improves quality.

Advantages of Agent Teams

When multiple AI agents cooperate, each with its own expert role, these limitations can be overcome.

Single AI

  • One model handles every task
  • Limited amount of information it can process
  • Tasks proceed sequentially
  • Only self-review is possible
  • Suited to simple tasks

Agent Team

  • Specialist agents divide the roles
  • Each agent keeps its own independent context
  • Multiple agents work in parallel
  • A dedicated review agent manages quality
  • Suited to complex projects

The core idea of an agent team is a human organizational structure carried over into the world of AI. Just as a company separates its planning, design, development, and QA teams, AI agents divide the roles and produce better results.

What is a Harness?

To make multiple AI agents move like a "team," you have to decide which role each one takes and in what order they work. What you need then is precisely a harness.

Definition of a Harness

A harness is a system for controlling and coordinating multiple AI agents. Put simply, it is the collection of rules, sequences, and role definitions that AI agents follow when they cooperate on a project.

The Metaphor: a Horse's Harness

In its original sense, the word harness refers to the strap gear fitted onto a horse. When a horse pulls a carriage, the harness serves as the guide that keeps it running in the right direction and arriving safely at the destination.

The same holds in the world of AI. If multiple AI agents each move according to their own judgment, confusion results. The harness hands each agent instructions such as "you take this role" and "when this task is finished, pass the results to that agent." Just as a horse's harness leads the horse in a safe direction, an AI harness leads its agents harmoniously and productively.

Structure of a Harness

Three Core Functions of a Harness

A harness guarantees the operation of an agent team through the following three core functions.

  1. Orchestration — Manages the flow of the overall job. It decides which agent to start first, what to run in parallel, and what to proceed sequentially. The orchestrator (usually the PM agent) plays the conductor.
  2. Handoff contracts — Define how and in what format work results are handed over between agents. Agreeing in advance on where agent B reads the file agent A finished, and in what format B outputs, reduces errors.
  3. Quality gates — At the end of each stage, check whether the output meets the criteria. A dedicated quality agent reviews the work separately to catch errors and requests revisions when anything falls short.
A harness is a kind of "rulebook" that tells AI agents "what to do" and "how to cooperate." Just as a physical harness guides a horse's movements, an AI harness coordinates the agents' actions and helps the project reach a successful completion.

The co-deck covered in this handbook is a working implementation of a multi-agent harness that operates in the Claude Desktop App environment. It consists of an 11-stage pipeline and automates the entire process, from research through presentation PDF output.

Expected Benefits

What concrete advantages come from using an AI agent team? Below are real-world use cases and their benefits.

Time Savings

The most intuitive benefit is a dramatic reduction in working time. For example, producing a 50-page market research report the traditional way — research, analysis, planning, writing, design — can take about 8 hours. With an agent team, however, work proceeds in parallel: while the research agent gathers material from the web, the design agent prepares templates and the storyline agent shapes the structure. As a result, the whole process can be shortened to roughly 45 minutes.

Of course, this is pure AI generation time and excludes the time a user spends reviewing and revising the AI's drafts. Even so, it is clear that the time spent on research, on drafting, and on applying designs can each be reduced.

Quality Improvement

Because agent teams divide specialized roles, you can expect expert-level output in each field. The research specialist concentrates on information retrieval and fact-checking, the design specialist maintains visual consistency, and the dedicated quality agent detects errors. This separation of producer and reviewer is a basic principle of quality improvement in human organizations too.

Reusability

An agent team built once can be reused in other projects. For example, once you have put together an agent-team configuration for "market research reports," the next report on a different topic can reuse the same structure as-is. If you save agent teams as templates for various purposes — handbook writing, presentation creation, code review — you never need to assemble one from scratch every time a new project starts.

Workflow

A typical workflow using an agent team looks like this:

1. Topic input — The user enters the project topic, the target audience, and the requirements.
2. Agent team starts automatically — The PM agent analyzes the topic and arranges suitable specialist agents sequentially or in parallel.
3. Deliverables generated — Each specialist agent performs its role, and the quality agent reviews the results.
4. Final deliverable provided — Once every stage is complete, an integrated final deliverable (report, presentation, handbook, etc.) is produced.
