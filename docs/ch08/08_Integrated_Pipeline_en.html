Combined co-consult + co-deck Pipeline

Learn the integrated workflow from consulting analysis to presentation: from topic input to a finished deck in 30-45 minutes using both co-consult and co-deck.

What This Chapter Covers
  • Why integrate co-consult and co-deck
  • Manual handoff method (report → presentation)
  • Full pipeline practice (topic → finished deck in 30-45 min)
  • Agent composition differences between the two systems
  • Tips for successful integration

Why Integrate co-consult and co-deck

Chapters 6 and 7 introduced you to two powerful variants: co-consult for consulting analysis and co-deck for presentation production. Each one is valuable on its own — but when you combine them, something remarkable happens. You can go from a raw topic to a fully researched, analytically grounded presentation in about 30-45 minutes.

The Natural Workflow

In real consulting practice, there is a natural progression: first you analyze, then you present. A consultant does not walk into a boardroom and start building slides before doing any research. Instead, the workflow looks like this:

  1. Research & Analyze — Gather information, identify key findings, structure the problem, and produce an analytical report.
  2. Summarize & Reformat — Extract the most important points from the report and reorganize them into a slide-friendly format.
  3. Design & Present — Turn the summarized content into a visually polished slide deck and deliver it as HTML or PDF.

This is exactly what the combined pipeline does. co-consult handles step 1 (research and analysis with its team of consulting specialists), and co-deck handles steps 2 and 3 (content structuring, design, and presentation generation with its production agents).

The Report-to-Slides Analogy

Think of it like writing a book and then creating a TED talk from it. The book (co-consult report) contains deep analysis, supporting data, and nuanced arguments. The TED talk (co-deck presentation) distills the key messages into a compelling visual narrative that audiences can absorb in 20 minutes. Both outputs are valuable, and each serves a different purpose — but they build on the same intellectual foundation.

co-consult Output

  • Detailed analysis report
  • Market landscape and competitive data
  • Problem structured as issue trees (MECE)
  • Strategic recommendations with evidence
  • Audience: analysts, managers, decision-makers

co-deck Output

  • Visual slide deck (HTML + PDF)
  • Key findings highlighted per slide
  • Narrative flow: problem → analysis → solution
  • Designed with theme, images, and diagrams
  • Audience: executives, clients, general audiences

Integrated Workflow Overview

Why not just use co-deck alone? co-deck has a built-in research agent that works well for general topics. However, co-consult brings specialized consulting agents — industry experts, strategy analysts, data analysts — that provide deeper, more structured analysis. When your topic requires business-grade strategic thinking (market analysis, competitive positioning, digital transformation), the co-consult analysis adds depth that a general-purpose research agent cannot match.

Manual Handoff Process

Currently, there is no automatic pipeline that connects co-consult output directly to co-deck input. The handoff between the two systems is manual — you, the human operator, perform the bridge. This might sound like extra work, but it is actually a valuable quality checkpoint. Let us walk through the process step by step.

What the Handoff Looks Like

The handoff is conceptually simple: you take the key findings from the co-consult report and feed them into co-deck as the starting point for the presentation. In practice, this means summarizing the report's core insights into a format that co-deck's storyline agent can work with.

  1. Complete the co-consult analysis. Run your topic through the full co-consult pipeline (Phases 0-6). Review the final report to ensure the analysis is complete, the recommendations are clear, and the evidence is well-organized.
  2. Copy the report content. Open the co-consult output report (typically in the project's output directory). Select the key sections: executive summary, main findings, strategic recommendations, and supporting data points. You do not need to copy the entire report — focus on the content you want to appear in the presentation.
  3. Summarize into slide-ready format. Create a brief summary document or directly paste the key content when co-deck's storyline agent asks for input. The goal is to provide co-deck with enough context to structure the slides without overwhelming it with raw report prose. Aim for 3-5 bullet points per slide topic.
  4. Start a new co-deck project. Open Claude Desktop App and navigate to the co-deck workspace. Begin Stage 0 by creating a new lecture profile. When prompted for the topic or research notes, paste your summarized co-consult findings as the starting content.
  5. Run the co-deck pipeline. Proceed through the standard 11-Stage pipeline (Stages 1-11). The storyline agent will use your summarized content to build the slide deck, the design agent will apply the visual theme, and the build/export agents will produce the final HTML and PDF.
Tip — Content Alignment: The most common handoff mistake is dumping the entire co-consult report verbatim into co-deck. Remember that a presentation is not a document. Summarize each key insight into one clear sentence or bullet point. Ask yourself: "If the audience can only remember three things from this slide, what should they be?" That is the content co-deck needs.
Future Automation: The manual handoff is a temporary step. Future versions of the ai-workspace-standards ecosystem plan to include an automated handoff contract that would allow co-consult to write its output in a format that co-deck can read directly. Until then, the manual process gives you full control over what content makes it into the presentation.

Handoff Quality Checklist

Before starting the co-deck pipeline, run through this quick checklist to ensure the handoff content is ready.

  1. Core message defined — Can you state the presentation's main message in one sentence?
  2. Key findings extracted — Have you identified the 3-5 most important findings from the co-consult report?
  3. Audience considered — Is the content appropriate for the presentation audience (executives, clients, students)?
  4. Data points selected — Have you chosen the most impactful data points and statistics to include?
  5. Recommendations prioritized — Are the action items and next steps clearly identified?

Full Pipeline Practice

Let us walk through a complete end-to-end example. We will use a realistic scenario: "Electric Vehicle Market Analysis for a Mid-size Auto Parts Manufacturer." The goal is to go from this topic to a finished 15-slide presentation in approximately 30-45 minutes.

Phase A: co-consult Analysis (approximately 15 minutes)

  1. Start co-consult and define the project.
    Open Claude Desktop App in the co-consult workspace. The PM agent initializes the project and asks about the engagement scope.

    Example conversation:
    You: "I need a market analysis of the electric vehicle industry for a mid-size auto parts manufacturer. They make braking systems and want to understand the EV transition landscape, competitive threats, and strategic opportunities."
    PM: "Understood. I will assemble the consulting team and begin research."
  2. Research and analysis.
    co-consult dispatches its specialized agents: the industry-expert gathers EV market data, the strategy-analyst evaluates competitive dynamics, and the sme (subject matter expert) assesses braking system technology trends.

    What happens: Each agent produces findings. The PM synthesizes them into a structured analysis with issue trees following the MECE framework (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive).
  3. Review the co-consult report.
    The PM presents the completed analysis. Review it for accuracy and completeness. Key sections should include market overview, competitive landscape, technology assessment, and strategic recommendations.

    Example output: A report with sections like "EV Market Growth Forecast," "Top 5 Competitors in EV Braking Systems," "Technology Gap Analysis," and "Strategic Recommendations for [Company]."

Phase B: Handoff (approximately 5 minutes)

  1. Summarize for slides.
    Extract the key points from the report and organize them into slide-ready summaries. Focus on the executive summary, the 3-5 most important findings, and the top recommendations.

    Example summary you might prepare:
    Topic: EV Market Entry Strategy for Auto Parts Manufacturer
    Key Finding 1: The global EV market is projected to grow at 22% CAGR through 2030
    Key Finding 2: Regenerative braking demand will increase 3x as EV adoption accelerates
    Key Finding 3: Two major competitors have announced EV-exclusive braking product lines
    Recommendation: Invest in regenerative braking R&D and establish partnerships with two EV OEMs

Phase C: co-deck Production (approximately 20 minutes)

  1. Initialize the co-deck project.
    Open Claude Desktop App in the co-deck workspace. Create a new presentation project. When the PM asks for topic details or research input, paste your summarized co-consult findings.

    Example conversation:
    PM: "What topic would you like for this presentation?"
    You: "EV Market Entry Strategy for Auto Parts Manufacturer. Here are the key findings from our consulting analysis: [paste summary]. Build a 15-slide deck targeting the company's executive team."
  2. Content creation and design.
    co-deck runs through Stages 1-8: research enrichment, storyline design, design spec creation, HTML build, image curation, and diagram generation. At Gate 2, review the proposed slide structure and approve it.

    What happens: The storyline agent creates a narrative flow (problem → analysis → solution). The design agent applies the visual theme. The html-build agent generates the HTML slides.
  3. Export and review.
    co-deck runs through Stages 9-11: layout measurement and PDF export. At Gate 5, review the sample PDF for layout and font quality. Approve the full PDF generation.

    Result: A complete 15-slide presentation in HTML and PDF format, visually themed, with key data points from the co-consult analysis presented as clear, audience-ready slides.
About Timing: The 30-45 minute estimate assumes you are already familiar with both co-consult and co-deck workflows (covered in Chapters 6 and 7). On your first attempt, allow 45-60 minutes. As you gain experience, the handoff becomes faster and the pipeline time decreases. Most of the time is spent waiting for the AI agents to complete their work — your active involvement is mainly reviewing and approving at each gate.

Agent Composition Differences

One of the most interesting aspects of comparing co-consult and co-deck is seeing how each variant assembles a different team of specialists tailored to its domain. Both share the same PM agent and the same orchestration framework, but the specialist agents are completely different. This is the power of the variant architecture — the same platform, different teams, different outputs.

Agent Comparison

co-consult Agents

  • pm — Project Manager (orchestrator)
  • industry-expert — Market and competitive insights
  • strategy-analyst — Strategic assessment and research
  • sme — Functional expertise and solution design
  • data-analyst — Statistical analysis and modeling
  • solutions-architect — Technical solution design
  • communications-lead — Strategic narrative and messaging
  • change-management-partner — Organizational transformation
  • workstream-lead — Delivery coordination
  • delivery-manager — Project delivery operations
  • technology-specialist — Digital transformation support

co-deck Agents

  • pm — Project Manager (orchestrator)
  • research — Web research and source collection
  • source-verifier — URL validation and trust scoring
  • storyline — Narrative design and slide composition
  • design — Visual design spec (colors, fonts, layout)
  • image-curator — Image search and download
  • diagram-specialist — SVG diagram and chart generation
  • html-build — HTML slide generation
  • measure — Layout measurement for PDF
  • pdf-export — PDF generation
  • version — File snapshot and restore

Shared vs. Unique Agents

Out of all the agents, only one is shared between the two variants: the PM (Project Manager). Everything else is domain-specific. This reflects a deliberate design choice — the PM provides the orchestration backbone, while each variant populates the team with specialists who have the right expertise for that domain.

Shared

  • pm — Orchestration, gate approvals, agent dispatch, quality enforcement

Domain-Difference Summary

  • co-consult agents are thinking-heavy: analysis, strategy, expert judgment
  • co-deck agents are production-heavy: content structuring, visual design, file generation
  • Neither set is "better" — they serve different stages of the same overall workflow
Contextual Differences: Even the shared PM agent behaves differently in each variant. In co-consult, the PM assembles a consulting team and manages a 7-Phase workflow focused on analysis quality. In co-deck, the PM manages an 11-Stage pipeline focused on production milestones and gate approvals. The PM's behavioral instructions are loaded from each variant's own CLAUDE.md or GEMINI.md, which override the shared base behavior with variant-specific rules.

Workflow Comparison

Beyond the agents themselves, the two variants use fundamentally different workflow structures.

co-consult Workflow

  • 7-Phase workflow (Phases 0-6)
  • Phase 0: Project initiation
  • Phase 1-2: Research and planning
  • Phase 3: Design and solution architecture
  • Phase 4: Execution and delivery
  • Phase 5: Lifecycle finalization
  • Phase 6: Quality assurance
  • Output: Analysis report (Markdown)

co-deck Workflow

  • 11-Stage pipeline (Stages 0-11)
  • Stage 0: Project configuration
  • Stage 1: Research
  • Stage 1.5: Source verification (optional)
  • Stage 2-3: Content and storyline
  • Stage 4: Design
  • Stage 3.5: Images and diagrams (parallel)
  • Stage 5-8: HTML build
  • Stage 9-10: Layout measurement
  • Stage 11: PDF export
  • Output: HTML slides + PDF

Success Tips

After running the combined pipeline a few times, you will develop your own rhythm. Here are some practical tips that will help you avoid common pitfalls and get the best results from the integration.

Tip 1: Run a Pre-Handoff Quality Check

Before starting the handoff, make sure the co-consult report passes a basic quality bar. A low-quality analysis will produce a low-quality presentation, no matter how good co-deck is at design.

  1. Is the executive summary clear? — Can you summarize the entire report in 2-3 sentences?
  2. Are the findings evidence-backed? — Does each key claim have supporting data?
  3. Are the recommendations actionable? — Can the audience actually act on them?
  4. Is the structure logical? — Does the report flow naturally from problem to analysis to solution?
Warning: Do not skip the co-consult review step. If the analysis has factual errors or logical gaps, those errors will propagate into the presentation. The co-deck agents will faithfully represent whatever content you feed them — they do not independently verify the accuracy of the consulting analysis.

Tip 2: Summarize, Do Not Copy-Paste

The handoff is not a copy-paste operation. A consulting report and a slide deck serve different communication purposes, and the content needs to be adapted.

The 3-Sentence Rule: For each slide topic, try to distill the co-consult findings into no more than 3 sentences. Each sentence becomes a bullet point on the slide. If you cannot express a finding in 3 sentences, it probably needs to be split across multiple slides or simplified. This discipline forces you to focus on what matters most.

Here is a practical approach to summarization:

  1. Read the full report section and highlight the key sentence in each paragraph.
  2. Group related highlights under slide topic headers (e.g., "Market Overview," "Competitive Landscape," "Technology Trends").
  3. Trim to essentials — remove jargon, qualifiers, and caveats that work in a report but clutter a slide.
  4. Add data callouts — note specific numbers, percentages, or comparisons that will make compelling slide content.

Tip 3: Pre-Select Theme and Style

During co-deck Stage 0, the PM will ask you to choose a rendering theme and visual style. Having these decisions made before you start the co-deck pipeline saves time and avoids back-and-forth.

Quick Decision Framework:
  • Business/corporate presentation? → Theme: pitch-enhanced, Style: premium-dark
  • Educational/lecture content? → Theme: outline, Style: academic
  • Creative/visual showcase? → Theme: zen, Style: visual-heavy
Check the full compatibility matrix in docs/html-themes/THEMES.md before confirming your selections.

Tip 4: Be Diligent with Gate Approvals

Both co-consult and co-deck have mandatory gates where you must explicitly approve before the pipeline advances. These gates exist for a reason — they are quality checkpoints that catch problems early.

co-consult Gates

  • Gate: Team Assembly — Review the proposed agent team and confirm it matches your engagement scope
  • Gate: Analysis Review — Review the completed analysis before finalization

co-deck Gates

  • Gate 2 (Mandatory) — Review storyline.md and slide_deck.md before design begins
  • Gate 5 (Mandatory) — Review the sample PDF before generating the full deck
Do Not Auto-Approve: It is tempting to quickly approve every gate to save time, especially on your second or third run. Resist this temptation. Each gate is a checkpoint where you can catch and fix issues before they become expensive downstream problems. Spending 2 minutes reviewing at Gate 2 can save 10 minutes of rework later.

Tip 5: Keep the Topic Focused

The combined pipeline works best when the topic is narrow and specific. A topic like "AI in Healthcare" is too broad for a 15-slide presentation that needs to show consulting-grade depth. A topic like "AI-Powered Diagnostic Tools for Radiology Departments" is much better — it gives both the consulting agents and the presentation agents a clear scope to work within.

The Goldilocks Zone: Aim for a topic that can be thoroughly analyzed in a 3-5 page consulting report and effectively presented in 12-18 slides. If you find yourself wanting 30+ slides, the topic is probably too broad — consider splitting it into two separate pipeline runs, each with its own focused theme.

Summary of Tips

Quick Reference
  • Quality first — Review the co-consult report before handoff
  • Summarize — Use the 3-Sentence Rule to distill findings
  • Pre-select — Choose theme and style before starting co-deck
  • Review gates — Take 2 minutes at each gate to prevent 10 minutes of rework
  • Stay focused — Narrow topics produce better results than broad ones
